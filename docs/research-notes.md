## 1. The Million Dollar Homepage (2005)

- **How it worked:** a 1000x1000 grid of one million pixels, sold in 10x10 blocks at $1 per pixel ($100 minimum). Each buyer supplied an image, a link and a hover tooltip. Alex Tew launched it on 26 Aug 2005 to pay for university. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Million_Dollar_Homepage
- **Result:** the first sale came on day 3. Revenue was about $250k by late September and 999,000 pixels had sold by 31 Dec 2005. The last 1,000 pixels went on eBay in Jan 2006 for **$38,100**. Gross revenue was **$1,037,100** over about 5 months (sold out in about 138 days), with an estimated $650–700k net. A DDoS-extortion attack in Jan 2006 took the site offline for about a week. (same source; https://www.techspot.com/article/2549-million-dollar-homepage/)
- **Why it worked:** the price was a one-off novelty and the canvas was finite. The "be part of internet history" story got press coverage, which drove more sales, and buyers advertised their own pixels. Scarcity was real: there was one page. Hundreds of copycats failed, including Tew's own Pixelotto, so the novelty could not be repeated (TechSpot).
- **Link rot:** about 22% of pixels were dead by 2014 (Quartz, via https://gizmodo.com/the-million-dollar-homepage-still-exists-but-a-fifth-o-1551899926). A Harvard LIL study in 2017 found that of 2,816 links, 547 were dead (342k pixels) and 489 redirected. The BBC put link rot at about 40% in 2019. A 2023 study found 27% loading cleanly, 45% redirecting and 28% returning errors (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_rot). The page still gets several thousand daily viewers.
- **Lesson:** content on a static plot decays. Plots need upkeep (renewals, health checks, "abandoned plot" states) or the map ends up looking like a graveyard.

## 2. Decentraland and The Sandbox (crypto land)

**Decentraland:** parcels cost about $20 at the 2017 launch and $6k–100k in the April 2021 surge. One plot sold for **$2.43M** in Nov 2021. Cumulative on-chain LAND volume to Mar 2025 was $184.5M across 37,424 sales. The median LAND sale was $813, the average about $4k, and the all-time-high single LAND sale was $434k (https://www.nonfungible.com/market-tracker/decentraland/LAND). The current floor is about **$130–$345** depending on tracker, undated (https://WWW.COINGECKO.COM/en/nft/decentraland). Users: in Oct 2022 DappRadar showed fewer than 1,000 daily transacting wallets, with one day at **38**. Decentraland claimed about 8,000 DAU (https://www.coindesk.com/web3/2022/10/11/dappradar-says-its-recalculating-decentraland-user-data). An unverified 2026 piece claims 847k monthly web uniques in Q4 2025. Reviewers consistently describe the world as empty, buggy and slow to load (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentraland). "Decentraland 2.0" desktop beta with quests and minigames launched in Oct 2024.

**The Sandbox:** the average LAND floor fell from **2.86 ETH (2021) to 0.13 ETH (2024), -95%** (CoinGecko analysis). A recent OpenSea floor is about **0.036 ETH**, with about 25.6k unique owners (https://opensea.io/collection/sandbox/explore). Average metaverse land prices were down 72% from highs as early as 2022 (https://cryptoslate.com/metaverse-land-prices-down-72-on-average-from-highs/). The 2021 headline sales were a $4.3M Republic Realm purchase and $450k paid to be Snoop Dogg's neighbour (well-known reports; not re-verified here). In **Aug 2025** Animoca removed the co-founders from executive roles and cut **more than half of about 250 staff**, citing AI efficiency. The company pivoted toward web3 apps and a meme-coin launchpad. SAND was down about 97% from its $8.40 peak (https://cointelegraph.com/news/the-sandbox-restructuring-ai-layoffs-cofounders-role-shift, https://blockworks.com/news/sandbox-co-founders-ousted-layoffs).

**Why these worlds are empty:**
1. Buyers were speculators, not players. Land was an asset with no use.
2. Owners had no reason to build: making 3D content is expensive and visitors were few.
3. Clients were heavy and slow, and needed a wallet.
4. Scarcity was artificial with no underlying demand, so prices fell once the token fell.
5. There was no daily loop.

**Meta context:** Horizon Worlds never passed a few hundred thousand MAU. Reality Labs lost $19.2B in 2025 (about $84B cumulative). In Mar 2026 Meta announced it would shut down Horizon Worlds VR, then reversed the decision within 48 hours; mobile is now the focus (https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/19/meta-decides-not-to-shut-down-horizon-worlds-on-vr-after-all/, https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2026/03/meta-kills-its-vr-metaverse-after-84-billion-in-losses/).

## 3. Earth 2 (fiat land on a real-world map), the closest analogue

- **Mechanics:** launched Nov 2020 from Brisbane by Shane Isaac. Earth is split into about **5.1 trillion 10x10m tiles** on a Google-Maps-like globe, and buyers pay by card (fiat). Tiles started at **$0.10**. Each country's price rose as tiles sold (dynamic per-country pricing), and some tiles soon passed $100. Landmark tiles resold for far above base, e.g. an NYC firehouse tile at $2,500 against a base of about $31 (https://domusweb.it/en/news/2021/03/02/all-eyes-on-san-marino-in-the-race-to-buy-virtual-land.html, https://cm.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2021053117135350081). Media headlines cited "Tokyo $1M / New York $2.5M" in aggregate holdings.
- **Growth driver:** referral commissions (a 5–10% cut) and YouTube "investor" content. One critic found about 62% of visits came from referrals and described the economy as reliant on new inflows (https://davidgerard.co.uk/blockchain/?p=22299). Earth 2 never published revenue or user totals. **(est.)** Revenue was in the tens of millions of USD.
- **Controversies:** it was widely labelled a "cash shop without a game" (https://www.pcgames.de/Spiele-Thema-239104/Specials/Scam-MMOs-Earth-2-Der-Cashshop-ohne-Spiel-1383430/). There was a 2021 defamation-threat drama with YouTubers (SidAlpha). Tier 1/Tier 2 land splits, an Essence token "promise" (EDC), marketplace freezes, and a 2024 retroactive airdrop all caused disputes (https://earth2.io/edc, https://earth2dotio.wordpress.com/2024/02/22/locked-countries-edc/). Trustpilot reviews complain of withdrawals pending for more than 2 months (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/earth2.io). **I found no evidence of a filed securities lawsuit or regulator action.**
- **Status 2025–26:** the game is still in "E2V1 Pre-Alpha", with teleportation added and a Nov 2025 pre-alpha update (https://x.com/earth2io). That is about 5 years of selling land before a playable game.
- **Lesson:** fiat land on a real map *can* sell at scale on speculation alone. But FlatMeta must avoid investment framing, referral-pyramid optics, resale "appreciation" promises and withdrawal and cash-out mechanics, all of which create regulatory and trust risk. Ship the play and decoration loop before selling scarcity.

## 4. Upland ("Monopoly on real addresses")

- **Mechanics:** you buy real street addresses as NFT deeds, collect UPX yield from properties, complete neighbourhood "collections" for bonus yield, build structures, and race or deliver goods along real streets. Cities open in phases; Miami added about 15k properties in Mar 2025 and **Sydney** joined in **May 2025** (https://metaversestreetjournal.com/2025/05/22/upland-expands-miami-and-unveils-sydney-collections-a-new-chapter-in-the-metaverse/).
- **Scale:** about 3M registered players and about **30k DAU** as of Oct 2023 (other sources say 4M), plus a $7M Series A extension with Animoca (https://decrypt.co/202498/crypto-game-upland-raises-7-million-ahead-ethereum-token-launch, https://gamesbeat.com/upland-raises-7m-for-virtual-land-web3-metaverse/).
- **Brand partners:** FIFA (multi-year deal, 2022), NFLPA, FC Porto, Spirit Halloween, Hyperloop (https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2021/05/12/2228743/0/en/Hyperloop-is-Coming-to-the-Real-World-Metaverse-Upland.html). Brands mostly come in as licensed collectibles or storefronts, not ads.
- **2025–26:** the company calls 2025 its "biggest year ever": 38 dev updates, Sparklet spendable in-game, and a real-time on-map PvP feature called "Sparklet Warz". Players voted to burn tokens (2.9M SPARKLET), and about 5.3B Sparklet is staked (https://uplandme.medium.com/2025-recap-inside-uplands-biggest-year-ever-53a6c4db94fa, https://egamers.io/upland-players-vote-to-burn-2-9m-sparklet-in-the-games-second-token-burn). No current DAU figure is public; **(est.)** it is a niche of tens of thousands. Upland survives because of its *game loops* (collections, yield, map events), not its land prices.

## 5. wplace.live (2025) and Reddit r/place

- **wplace.live:** launched **21 Jul 2025** by Brazilian devs (Murilo Matsubara, Enzo Watanabe). It is a pixel canvas of about 4 trillion pixels laid over the real world map, with a Google login. It reached **more than 1M users in 4 days** and **more than 10.6M users by September 2025** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wplace, http://www.milwaukeeindependent.com/newswire/malaysia-milwaukee-wplace-invites-millions-use-world-map-canvas-paint-pixel-art/).
  - **Loop:** players start with 30 pixel charges and regain 1 every 30s. Each pixel earns 1 droplet and each level-up earns 500. Droplets buy more charges, faster regeneration, premium colours (32 of the 63 colours cost 2,000 droplets each) and **country flags**, which refund 10% of pixels placed in that country.
  - **Money:** droplet packs cost **$5–$100**, roughly 5,000 droplets per $1 (e.g. 25k droplets for $5) (https://wplacetool.org/blog/guide/droplets-complete-guide). A gray-market resale scene exists (FunPay). The developer posted publicly about "pay to win" criticism, bans and downtime (27 Aug 2025).
  - **Virality drivers:** it is your own city or house on the map; national and streamer "wars" (e.g. a Spanish YouTuber's "conquer Andorra" raid); TikTok and Reddit timelapses; a free start.
  - **Problems:** servers collapsed under load (leaderboards and signups broke), griefing and voiding, bots, and political and hate art (Gaza, trans-flag fights near J.K. Rowling's house, territorial claims) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wplace). There is no 2026 usage data; **(est.)** it is well below its peak.
- **Reddit r/place:** 2017 had about 1M participants and about 16M tiles. **2022** had about **10.4M participants** and more than 160M pixels, peaking at about 5.9M pixels per hour (https://www.mediaweek.com.au/10-4-million-people-get-involved-as-r-place-returns-to-reddit, https://www.statista.com/statistics/1360668/reddit-r-places-engagement). 2023 had no official totals, and the event drew community anger and profanity protests (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R/place). It is a time-boxed event with deliberate scarcity (1 pixel per 5 min) and tribal coordination via subreddits and Discords.
- **Lesson:** a map that is *temporary, contested and cheap* goes viral. A map that is *permanent and owned* builds identity. FlatMeta can combine both: permanent plots plus seasonal "events" or contested layers.

## 6. Location-based games and contribution systems

- **Pokémon GO:** Scopely bought Niantic's games for **$3.5B** (Mar 2025). Pokémon GO has about **20M weekly players** (about half play daily), and all Niantic titles together have more than 30M MAU. Gross spend in 2024 was more than $8B lifetime per Scopely's framing, and AppMagic estimates about **$9.1B** in store revenue over 10 years. Store revenue has declined for 5 years, but the web shop and sponsorships offset this (https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokmon-go-has-20m-weekly-active-players-nearly-nine-years-after-its-global-launch, https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-go-celebrates-10th-anniversary-with-over-9bn-in-revenue/). **Sponsored locations:** cost-per-visit **under $0.50 per daily unique visitor** (Niantic, 2017). McDonald's Japan used about 3,000 stores (est. $0.9–3M/day at peak), and Starbucks used 7,800 US stores (https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/31/pokemon-go-sponsorship-price). This is a proven model: brands pay for footfall to real places.
- **Foursquare/Swarm:** mayorships and badges drove the 2010–13 boom. Splitting the app into Swarm in 2014 removed the game and usage fell sharply ("ghost town"). Mayorships came back in 2015 as a 30-day check-in rule (https://www.engadget.com/2015/06/22/swarm-foursquare-mayor/). City Guide shut down on 15 Dec 2024 (web: 28 Apr 2025), and Foursquare now runs as a data/B2B business (https://foursquare.com/city-guide-sunset/). Lesson: status layers attached to places are powerful, but fragile if removed.
- **Google Maps Local Guides:** 10 levels, from Level 2 at 15 points to Level 10 at 100,000 points. A review earns 10 points and a photo 5. A badge appears from Level 4 (250 points) (https://support.google.com/maps/answer/6225851?hl=en). There were **150M Local Guides by about 2021** (https://searchengineland.com/google-now-50-million-local-guides-adding-content-google-maps-search-284737). Perks are mostly status, with small early perks (Play credits). Points plus levels plus a public badge make people contribute for free at enormous scale.
- **GeoGuessr:** a bootstrapped Swedish company. Revenue was **SEK 311M (about $29M) in 2024, up 33%**, and **SEK 471.5M (about $45M) in 2025, up 52%**, with about 40% operating margin and 79 staff (registry data via https://search.vainu.com/company/geoguessr-ab-omsattning-och-nyckeltal/SE5569398349/foretagsinfo). It went fully paid in early 2024. It has 80M+ cumulative players and a $400M–1B valuation (https://dealroom.co/companies/geoguessr/). Pro is about $2–4/month **(est., check current price)**. It proves that "real-world map as game" plus subscription plus esports and streamer culture is a strong business.

## 7. Map and brand advertising

- **Waze Ads:** CPM about $2. The minimum is about $2/day ($60/month), and one-location businesses were capped at about $3,000/month in 2018. Branded pins carry hours and a phone number; zero-speed takeovers need a budget of at least $100/day (https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-it-cost-to-advertise-on-waze/, https://marketingdive.com/news/waze-extends-local-ad-program-for-small-businesses/520252). Waze Local drove a 57% lead lift in one dealer case (https://searchengineland.com/case-study-waze-local-ads-drove-a-57-increase-in-leads-to-local-auto-dealer-303117).
- **Google Maps promoted pins:** run through Google Ads local campaigns. Advertisers pay per interaction (directions, call, website), with no published Maps-specific CPC. Google Search averages about $2.69 CPC (2026) (https://www.shopify.com/blog/google-maps-ads).
- **Snap Map:** reaches **400M+ monthly users** (2025). "Promoted Places" were tested with McDonald's and Taco Bell in Oct 2024 and went generally available at NewFronts 2025. Snap reports a +17.6% visitation lift for "Top Picks" (https://newsroom.snap.com/say-it-in-a-snap-newfronts-2025).
- **Roblox/Fortnite:** a custom branded experience costs **up to about $500k to build** on Roblox, plus live-ops (https://forerunnerventures.substack.com/p/the-cq-should-your-brand-be-in-the). UEFN islands run "mid six figures" (https://www.influencers-time.com/fortnite-creative-playbook-islands-costs-and-real-roi/). Roblox had about 240 brand activations and about $80M in brand spend in 2023 (Naavik). Cost per play ranges from $1 to fractions of a cent.
- **Takeaway:** pricing has two tiers. SMBs pay **$60–$3,000/month** for map visibility. Big brands pay **$100k–$500k+** per showcase build or activation, and per-visit CPV is **$0.15–$0.50**.

## 8. AI market context (2025–2026)

- **Answer engines:** ChatGPT had **800M weekly users** in Oct 2025 (https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/06/sam-altman-says-chatgpt-has-hit-800m-weekly-active-users/), and about 900M is reported in early 2026 (secondary sources). Google AI Overviews reach 2B+ monthly users, and AI Mode reportedly reached more than 1B monthly by May 2026 (third-party; https://getairefs.com/blog/google-ai-overviews-statistics-june-2026/). In Pew's Jul 2025 study, users clicked a link on only **8%** of searches that showed an AI Overview (https://www.searchenginejournal.com/people-aren-t-leaving-google-for-ai-they-re-using-both/588200/).
- **Local discovery via AI:** in BrightLocal's 2026 survey, the share of consumers using ChatGPT or other AI for local business recommendations went from **6% to 45%** in a year, making it the #3 source; use of Google reviews fell from 83% to 71% (https://www.brightlocal.com/research/lcrs-ai-trust/). "GEO" (generative engine optimization) is now a line item for SMBs: they need structured, citable, up-to-date facts that agents can read. A plot mini-page with schema.org data plus an MCP/API endpoint fits this need directly.
- **MCP:** donated to the Linux Foundation's **Agentic AI Foundation in Dec 2025**. Monthly SDK downloads went from about 100k in Nov 2024 to about **97M by Mar 2026**. Server counts vary by registry: about 6.4k in the official registry and 10k–17k indexed elsewhere (https://byteiota.com/model-context-protocol-97m-installs-standard-wins-2/, https://thenextweb.com/news/rise-of-model-context-protocol-in-the-agentic-era). Shipping an MCP server is now normal practice, though discovery among thousands of servers is the hard part.
- **Content saturation:** about **50% of new web articles are AI-generated** (Graphite: crossed in Nov 2024 and has plateaued at about half), yet **86% of top Google results are still human-written** (https://www.axios.com/2025/10/14/ai-generated-writing-humans). As content becomes cheap, value moves to what cannot be generated:
  - verified identity and provenance
  - real places and presence
  - scarce, finite positions (a plot that only one person owns)
  - community and status
  - attention
  
  The AI plot designer is a convenience; the product's moat is a scarce real-world position plus a social layer.
- **Link-in-bio comparable (Linktree):** 50M+ users by Jan 2025, with about 70M profiles claimed in 2026 (one source). Revenue was about $37M in 2023 and about $61–64M in 2024–25 **(est., Sacra)**. Pricing after the Nov 2025 change: Free / Starter $8 / Pro $15 / Premium $35 per month, with a 12% commerce fee on the free tier (https://u2l.ai/blog/linktree-pricing). Plot mini-pages compete here; the differentiator is the *location* and the map discovery.

- **wplace.live** (Jul 2025): the biggest new "own a piece of the world map" hit, with 10.6M users in about 6 weeks.
- **The Sandbox:** restructured in Aug 2025 (more than half of staff cut, founders sidelined, pivot away from the metaverse).
- **Meta Horizon Worlds:** Reality Labs cut more than 1,000 jobs in Jan 2026; VR shutdown announced and reversed in Mar 2026; now mobile-first.

## Implications for FlatMeta

1. **Positioning:** pitch FlatMeta as a "living, social map of the real world that you decorate and that AI agents can read". Avoid "virtual real estate" and "investment" language entirely. Decentraland, Sandbox and Earth 2 show that speculation-led land empties out and draws scam accusations.
2. **No cash-out:** don't offer withdrawals, resale profit or "appreciation" messaging. Earth 2's referral and withdrawal complaints are the trap to avoid. If you allow plot transfers, run them as a platform-fee marketplace in credits, or don't allow them at all at launch.
3. **Pricing anchor for individuals:** the Million Dollar Homepage charged $1/pixel ($100 minimum); Earth 2 charged $0.10 to $100+ per tile by country demand; wplace charges $5–$100 packs. Suggested range: **$1–$10 entry plots** (impulse buy), **tiered by country or region demand** (Earth 2's model, but published and transparent), and **$25–$250 premium or landmark plots**. Treat these as hypotheses to test.
4. **Subscription anchors:** use Linktree's $8/$15/$35 and GeoGuessr's ~$2–4/month **(est.)** consumer tiers. **Business plots at $15–$49/month** sit between link-in-bio pricing and Waze's $60/month minimum. Brand showcase plots can price against Waze Plus ($100/day ≈ $3k/month) and Roblox builds ($100k–$500k), so **$1k–$10k/month or a campaign fee** is defensible for featured placement plus an AI-built showcase.
5. **Sell outcomes to brands:** sponsor footfall and events, not just pixels. Pokémon GO's CPV (<$0.50 per visit) and Snap's +17.6% visit lift are the proof points. A "visit / check-in at this plot's real place" quest is a measurable product.
6. **Ship the loop before scarcity:** Earth 2 sold land for 5 years without a game. Day-one loops should be: decorate (AI designer), visit and like, quests and XP, weekly leaderboards by country and city, and neighbour interactions.
7. **Borrow wplace's virality engine:** free-to-start charges or energy, country and city teams, time-boxed "events" or contested layers on top of permanent plots, and shareable timelapses and screenshots for TikTok. Expect load spikes; design for 10x surges.
8. **Borrow Local Guides and Foursquare status systems:** points per action, 10 public levels, badges, and recurring "mayor of [city/block]" titles. Never remove status mechanics once users invest in them (Swarm's mistake).
9. **Fight link rot:** require periodic renewal or activity, run automated link and image health checks, and show "dormant" visual states. The Million Dollar Homepage had about 40% rot by 2019; an empty-looking map kills retention.
10. **Moderation is a launch requirement:** a real-world map attracts political, hate and harassment content near real homes, as wplace showed with art near J.K. Rowling's house. Use AI image and text moderation on upload, apply stricter rules for residential areas, provide report and appeal flows, and add bot detection.
11. **AI angle (the GEO wedge):** 45% of consumers now ask AI for local recommendations. Sell the plot mini-page as the business's agent-readable profile (schema.org, llms.txt, MCP tools like `find_plots_near`, `get_business_info`). This is a real SMB pain point with budget behind it.
12. **MCP is table stakes, not a moat:** there are thousands of servers. Distribution comes from listings in the official registry and clients, plus a clear use case ("ask Claude what's on this block").
13. **Scarcity is the product, but keep it honest:** in an AI-content glut, a unique real-world position plus identity plus community is what stays scarce. Make each plot singular and attributable. Use verified-business badges and human-made or provenance flags.
14. **Comparable business benchmarks:** GeoGuessr (map game, ~$45M revenue, 40% margin, bootstrapped) shows a map game can be a healthy business without speculation. Upland (~30k DAU) shows the ceiling of token-led land games. wplace (10M+ users in weeks, thin monetisation) shows how much attention is available and how hard it is to convert.
15. **Main uncertainty:** no public data shows that *non-crypto, fiat-only* map-plot ownership sustains recurring revenue. Earth 2 is the only scaled example, and its demand was speculative. Validate early by measuring repeat visits per plot and paid renewal rates, not first-purchase spikes.
